\begin{abstract}
SVG provenance marks stored in comments, metadata, or numeric serialization can disappear under routine optimization. We study a different boundary: an SVG generator proposes visually comparable geometric alternatives, and a keyed sampler chooses among them before a contour enters the document. The released artifact carries evidence in visible geometry rather than an added XML field. Our candidate-assisted construction uses keyed Gumbel-max sampling, an authenticated private trace, arc-length geometric matching, and an exact Poisson-binomial null calculation. A model-neutral categorical sampler also identifies a concrete integration boundary for OmniSVG's discrete coordinate tokens. In three completed 16-contour local-model trials, all keyed choices survived Scour, SVGO, two-decimal path rounding, reordering, bounded translations, and four-path deletion under the conditional erasure model, with null probability $2^{-16}$ before deletion. Direct metadata and numeric-LSB baselines fail under SVGO and rounding, respectively. These are prototype results, not a superiority claim: keyless control-point perturbations eventually defeat detection, a manifest-free centroid ablation has inadequate power, and no method can watermark every SVG under strict fidelity when an asset has zero admissible choice capacity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
An SVG is both a program and a picture. Equivalent renderings may use different whitespace, command syntax, curve subdivisions, group structure, or numeric precision. This makes file-level provenance fragile and makes semantic robustness difficult: the verifier should follow visible geometry without accepting a hidden marked decoy.

\system{} moves the watermark operation into generation. At drawing step $t$, an upstream model supplies alternatives $C_t=\{c_{t,1},\ldots,c_{t,m_t}\}$ with probabilities $q_{t,i}$. The alternatives and their weights are fixed independently of the secret key. A keyed draw selects one alternative before SVG assembly, and the model may condition subsequent generation on that selection. This is an adaptation of inference-time text watermarking's sampling boundary to structured vector geometry, not a claim that text-watermark algorithms directly solve SVG verification.

Our prototype contributes: (1) a distribution-preserving keyed categorical sampler; (2) a candidate-assisted geometric verifier robust to tested serialization changes; (3) a manifest-free ablation with an exact idealized power calculation and recorded negative results; (4) a provider-neutral token-sampling core and an OmniSVG 1.1 coordinate-token policy; and (5) reproducible baseline and attack scripts. Novelty remains subject to a wider patent and systems search.

\section{Threat model and claims}
The generator and verifier know a key $K$. An attacker sees the SVG but not $K$ or the authenticated candidate trace. Benign processors may round numbers, rewrite path commands, reorder paths, or merge path elements. Adversaries may delete, translate, simplify, redraw, rasterize, or replace geometry. We report survival only for named attacks and do not claim robustness to unrestricted quality-preserving rewriting.

Candidate-assisted evidence requires a private HMAC-authenticated trace. A deployment additionally needs a pre-generation commitment to the asset identifier and trace hash; otherwise a key holder can construct evidence after seeing an SVG. The current prototype does not provide this timestamp service.

The verifier accepts a strict rendering subset containing inline-painted paths and groups. It rejects transforms, CSS indirection, masks, filters, text, images, and unsupported elements. This fail-closed policy prevents marked but invisible geometry from authenticating unrelated visible content.

\section{Keyed geometric sampling}
Let $F(c)$ be a fingerprint of uniformly spaced arc-length samples of a contour. Domain-separated HMAC defines pseudorandom uniforms
\[
u_{t,i}=\operatorname{PRF}_K(\mathit{asset},t,F(c_{t,i}))\in(0,1).
\]
The selected index is
\[
J_t=\arg\max_i\left\{\log q_{t,i}-\log(-\log u_{t,i})\right\}.
\]
Under independent uniform $u_{t,i}$, the Gumbel-max identity gives $\Pr[J_t=i]=q_{t,i}$. Thus the keyed mechanism has the same categorical marginal as the upstream choice in the ideal-PRF experiment. Candidate quality and independence from $K$ remain obligations of the adapter.

The verifier authenticates the trace, samples released contours by arc length, and uses minimum-cost assignment to recorded generation steps. Shape comparison removes a bounded per-path translation while centroid drift constrains synchronization. Each step's acceptance radius is 45\% of its nearest candidate separation (or the configured minimum), leaving disjoint regions and a 10\% rejection gap. Let $R$ be recognized steps and $r_t=q_{t,J_t}$ the keyed winner probability. We report
\[
p_{\rm cond}=\Pr\left[\sum_{t\in R} B_t\ge M\right],\qquad B_t\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(r_t).
\]
This conditional test requires erasure/recognition to be independent of the keyed winner. We also report a conservative tail over all original steps, treating erasures as failures. Neither statistic is a probability of authorship.

\section{Capacity limit}
Let $\mathcal A_\epsilon(x)$ be the set of outputs admissible under a fidelity relation around asset $x$.

\paragraph{Proposition.} Any choice-based watermark respecting that relation has per-asset payload at most $\log_2|\mathcal A_\epsilon(x)|$ bits.

\paragraph{Argument.} The encoder can distinguish at most one message per admissible output. An injective message-to-output map therefore contains at most $|\mathcal A_\epsilon(x)|$ messages. Taking a base-two logarithm gives the bound. If $|\mathcal A_\epsilon(x)|=1$, capacity is zero.

This elementary bound rules out a nonzero universal guarantee under strict equivalence. Text, embedded raster images, filters, and rigid primitives can have model-specific alternatives, but those alternatives must be defined and verified. A path watermark cannot authenticate a mixed-content document as a whole.

\section{Manifest-free ablation}
Our ablation quantizes each contour's arc-length centroid into a symbol $z$, rejects guard-band boundaries, and selects the alternative with largest keyed $U_K(z)$. For $m$ equal-weight alternatives, the selected value is $U\sim\mathrm{Beta}(m,1)$ and $-\log U\sim\mathrm{Exp}(m)$. Across $n$ independent contours,
\[
S=\sum_i-\log U_i\sim
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{Gamma}(n,1), & H_0,\\
\mathrm{Gamma}(n,m), & H_1.
\end{cases}
\]
At false-positive rate $10^{-3}$ for $m=2$, idealized power is approximately 0.220, 0.696, and 0.990 for $n=16,32,64$. A fixed-key 32-contour fixture in our suite yields $p=0.0558$ and is not detected. Small translations also change centroid cells. We therefore treat this as a negative ablation rather than the primary system.

\section{Model adapters}
The framework-neutral sampler consumes the exact categorical logits after the model's grammar mask and truncation. An adapter must prove that the current candidates all represent the same semantic token class, pass the complete truncated set, and map selected tokens back to canonical geometry.

OmniSVG 1.1 is our reference target because its public tokenizer separates command, coordinate, color, and arc-parameter regions. The conservative policy marks coordinate tokens only: IDs 151944--191946 for 4B and 152072--192075 for 8B. It declines a step if any truncated candidate lies outside that range. A grammar state machine and geometry-resynchronizing detector remain necessary for a watermarked OmniSVG run. The official release reports roughly 17~GB GPU memory for 4B and targets CUDA, while our host exposes 16~GB Metal memory; we did not download the checkpoint merely to claim an unsupported local run. We did execute the official hosted 4B option: its 3,381-byte one-path output passes our strict carrier assessor after admitting the producer's inert \texttt{filling} metadata. The public endpoint exposes only the completed SVG, so this validates output compatibility rather than inference-time insertion.

The same sampler can serve path-token models such as IconShop, but their token map and verifier are separate adapter work. Generality belongs to the sampling theorem and interface, not to an assertion that one token range works across models.

\section{Prototype evaluation}
The implementation has 38 passing tests. They cover wrong keys, distribution preservation, hidden paths, rendering-indirection rejection, mixed-content and OmniSVG-output assessment, Scour, SVGO, path reorder and merge, deletion, adaptive radii, and bounded translation.

We connected a local Qwen 3.5 4B model through a structured control-point proposal interface. For each of three layouts (radial flower, parallel wave, and leaf), it generated two alternatives for each of 16 cubic contours; the keyed winner was returned to the generator before the next step. All 16 choices verify in every asset, giving $p=1.5259\times10^{-5}$. Table~\ref{tab:baseline} summarizes the common outcome of same-asset comparisons.

Path reorder and translations up to one canvas unit preserve all 16 matches after synchronization. Deleting four paths leaves 12 recognized matches: the conditional statistic remains significant at $2^{-12}$, while the conservative all-step statistic is $0.0384$ and does not pass threshold 0.01.

Our adaptive keyless attack retains every endpoint but moves the two cubic control points in opposite directions. With disjoint per-step radii and the conditional statistic, the first tested failing amplitudes are 12 units for flower, 6 for wave, and 4 for leaf. Their sampled-geometry RMS values are 0.754\%, 0.375\%, and 0.266\% of the canvas diagonal; at 512 pixels, librsvg gives normalized pixel MAE 3.56\%, 8.48\%, and 2.31\%. The immediately smaller tested amplitudes remain detected. The conservative statistic rejects earlier because it treats erasures as failures. Pixel error is not a perceptual-equivalence judgment, and conditional calibration depends on the erasure assumption. The result demonstrates a working real-generator path and a measurable removal boundary; it does not establish broad superiority.

\section{Related work}
Blinova and Urbanovich encode SVG data through cubic B\'ezier subdivision~\cite{blinova}. Kwon et al. use rational-curve invariants and identify degree reduction as a failure mode~\cite{kwon}. Nakai and Kohara embed in Haar coefficients of sampled vector drawings~\cite{nakai}. Huber et al. bound perturbations that preserve planar topology~\cite{huber}. SynthID-Text demonstrates scalable inference-time keyed text sampling~\cite{synthid}; our geometric proposal boundary is an adaptation. General results show limits on strong watermarking under quality and perturbation-oracle assumptions~\cite{impossibility}.

\section{Limitations and next experiments}
A submission-quality evaluation still requires hundreds or thousands of licensed assets, an executed inference-time hook inside a specialist generator such as OmniSVG, human or perceptual quality assessment, calibrated false positives at $10^{-4}$, rasterize/revectorize attacks, simplification and cropping, topology checks, and adaptive attackers with render-quality oracles. Baselines need faithful implementations of prior curve-domain methods, not only metadata and numeric LSB. Public precommitment and key rotation also need a protocol evaluation.

\section{Reproducibility}
The repository contains the implementation, tests, local-model and hosted-specialist adapters, attack scripts, machine-readable reports, and this source. Private keys are excluded. The working tree occupies approximately 197~MB; no model weights were added by the project.
